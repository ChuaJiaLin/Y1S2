# Reflection

The Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) course provided valuable knowledge and practical experience in user experience (UX) design principles, interaction design processes, and usability evaluation techniques in interactive system development. It established a strong foundation for designing systems that are efficient, user-friendly, and aligned with user needs.

Throughout the course, comprehensive understanding was developed in areas such as human behaviour, cognitive principles, user-centred design, prototyping, usability testing, and evaluation methods. These concepts were reinforced through lectures, assignments, and project-based learning activities, ensuring both theoretical knowledge and practical application.

The hands-on prototype development project significantly enhanced creativity, analytical thinking, and problem-solving skills. Practical exposure to UX design processes, including requirements gathering, conceptual design, sketching, prototyping, and user evaluation, strengthened the ability to design effective and interactive systems based on user feedback.

In addition, teamwork, communication, and collaboration skills were improved through group discussions, peer assessments, presentations, and iterative development processes. These activities encouraged continuous improvement, idea sharing, and effective communication of design decisions.

The course also increased awareness of the importance of developing accessible, user-friendly, and efficient systems that enhance overall user satisfaction. This highlights the role of user-centred design in creating successful digital products and applications.

Overall, the course successfully enhanced both theoretical understanding and practical competency in human-computer interaction and UX design, while strengthening confidence in applying these skills in real-world system development.
